# Marketplace Micro-Product Operating Playbook

Reference for staying focused on fast, evidence-driven, AI-assisted product creation

Source: [original DOCX](references/micro_product_marketplace_operating_playbook.docx). Transcribed on 4 October 2026. The source wording, tables, and original current-position snapshot are preserved below; the subsequent [opportunity review](research/2026-10-04-opportunity-review.md) records the research findings.

## 1 Objective

We are not trying to build one big startup. We are building a portfolio of small marketplace products that can generate recurring or repeatable revenue with low ongoing effort.

- Small scope
- Existing demand before coding
- Built-in marketplace distribution
- Fast delivery
- Low maintenance
- Business users with willingness to pay
- AI handles most repeatable engineering work

## 2 Primary Ecosystems

1. Atlassian Marketplace — primary focus because of B2B users, marketplace discovery, and strong monetization.
2. Shopify App Store — huge merchant market with many painful operational problems.
3. JetBrains Marketplace — developer tools fit our skills and can often be very small.

Initial attention split: Atlassian 40% · Shopify 35% · JetBrains 25%. Change this when evidence says otherwise.

## 3 Roles

### Your role

- Approve or reject opportunities.
- Handle platform accounts, billing, identity, and marketplace ownership.
- Review UI/UX and critical code.
- Test products as a real user.
- Make final product decisions.

### AI assistant role

- Research marketplaces and competitors.
- Read reviews, complaints, and feature requests.
- Rank opportunities using evidence.
- Design product scope and architecture.
- Break work into tasks.
- Generate most code, tests, docs, and release material.
- Help debug and review.
- Recommend pricing and launch structure.
- Tell us when to improve, maintain, or kill a product.
- Give a clear next action instead of leaving us wondering what to do.

## 4 Core Workflow

1. Market research
2. Opportunity discovery
3. Validate demand
4. Score the idea
5. Build a brutally small MVP
6. Submit for marketplace approval
7. Launch
8. Observe installs, usage, and revenue
9. If traction: improve
10. If weak: kill or park

## 5 What Counts as Evidence

- Existing paid competitors.
- Popular products with weak ratings or repeated complaints.
- Marketplace search demand.
- Repeated feature requests.
- Forum or community complaints.
- Workaround tutorials showing the platform is missing something.
- Expensive products solving a technically simple workflow.

Rule: No product gets built before we can point to real evidence that people already have the problem.

## 6 Opportunity Signals

| Signal | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Popular but poorly reviewed | Demand is proven; execution is weak. |
| Simple but expensive | Potential room for a smaller, cleaner product. |
| Repeated feature request | A missing feature can become a standalone product. |
| Bloated incumbent | Build the one-feature version. |
| Missing integration/workflow | Narrow use cases can still support paying users. |

## 7 Scoring Model

| Factor | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Existing demand | 25% |
| Competition weakness | 20% |
| Build simplicity | 15% |
| Willingness to pay | 15% |
| Marketplace discoverability | 10% |
| Maintenance burden | 10% |
| AI-buildability | 5% |

Decision rule: Ideas below our agreed threshold are ignored, even if they sound exciting.

## 8 MVP Discipline

- One painful workflow first.
- No unnecessary dashboards.
- No generic AI assistant unless essential to the workflow.
- No extra integrations until users prove they need them.
- No polishing for weeks before revenue proof.

Target build times: Tiny ~1 day · Normal utility ~2–4 days · More complex ~5–10 days.

## 9 Revenue Milestones

$1 → $10 → $100 MRR → $500 MRR → $1,000 MRR → $3,000 MRR

First priority is proof, not scale. The first paying stranger matters more than a complicated growth plan.

## 10 Portfolio Model

Treat each product as a small investment of development time. We do not need every product to win.

- A few products may make nothing.
- Some may make small recurring revenue.
- One or two may become meaningful earners.
- Revenue decides where more time goes.

## 11 Product Rules

- Prefer existing demand over originality.
- Avoid products that require educating the market.
- Prefer business users over consumers.
- Competition is acceptable when customers are visibly unhappy.
- Prefer boring operational problems.
- Prefer products that save time, reduce risk, or remove repeated manual work.
- Keep maintenance burden low.
- Do not become emotionally attached to an idea.

## 12 What We Avoid

- Generic AI wrappers.
- Another todo app, generic CRM, generic form builder, or generic chatbot.
- Consumer products needing massive free-user volume.
- Advertising as the main monetization model.
- Three-month builds before demand is validated.
- Building first and hoping distribution appears later.

## 13 AI Development Model

AI handles repeatable work: research → spec → architecture → scaffolding → implementation → tests → docs → marketplace listing → release notes.

Your value is judgment: catch bad code, validate UX, make trade-offs, and decide what deserves more time.

## 14 Weekly Operating Rhythm

- Day 1 — research and select opportunity.
- Day 2 — build core product.
- Day 3 — finish and test.
- Day 4 — marketplace submission.
- Day 5 — research the next opportunity.

Desired pipeline: Product A in review · Product B in development · Product C in research.

## 16 Reset Checklist — Use This When We Feel Off Track

- Are we solving a problem with real evidence of demand?
- Is this still small enough to test quickly?
- Are we adding features before users are paying?
- Are we relying on external marketing instead of marketplace demand?
- Is this useful to a business user who can pay?
- Can AI remove most of the implementation burden?
- Is maintenance likely to stay reasonable?
- Would we still build this if we had only 3 days?
- If this shows no traction, are we willing to kill it?
- What is the single highest-value next action today?

NORTH STAR: Existing demand + weak solution + small scope + fast build + marketplace distribution.
